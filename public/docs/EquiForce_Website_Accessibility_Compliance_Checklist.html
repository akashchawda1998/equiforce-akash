How to use this checklist

Work through Part 1 (program actions) to stand up the compliance effort, then use Part 2 (technical criteria) as the audit instrument for each page template. Mark each success criterion Pass, Fail or N/A, and record the owner and remediation ticket in the notes column. Retain completed copies — dated evidence of a good-faith, documented remediation effort is itself a meaningful legal protection.

Note on the standard: there is no federal regulation that names a technical standard for private businesses under ADA Title III. WCAG 2.1 Level AA is the benchmark U.S. courts, the Department of Justice, and settlement agreements consistently apply, and is the appropriate target.

Part 1 — Program and Governance Actions

# Action Detail Status
A. SCOPE AND BASELINE
1 Define the digital estate in scope Inventory everything the public or a client can reach: equiforce.ai marketing site, any client login/reporting portal, the product UI, documentation, careers page and applicant tracking system, email templates, and downloadable PDFs/XLSX. Accessibility obligations do not stop at the marketing site. Done
2 Adopt WCAG 2.1 Level AA as the written standard Record the decision in a short internal policy. Courts and DOJ treat 2.1 AA as the benchmark; WCAG 2.2 AA is a reasonable forward-looking target if you are rebuilding anyway. Done
3 Commission a baseline audit Automated scan (axe DevTools, WAVE, Lighthouse, Siteimprove) plus manual expert review. Automated tools catch roughly 30–40% of issues; keyboard and screen-reader testing catches the rest. Done
4 Manual keyboard and screen-reader testing Test every template with keyboard only, then with NVDA + Firefox, JAWS + Chrome, VoiceOver + Safari (desktop and iOS), and TalkBack on Android. Done
5 Test the top-traffic and conversion paths first Homepage, services/solutions pages, pricing, contact and demo-request forms, careers, and any gated content flow. These are the pages plaintiff firms scan. Done
B. REMEDIATION
6 Triage findings by severity and legal exposure Blockers (keyboard traps, unlabeled forms, missing alt text, contrast failures) first; cosmetic issues later. Rank by whether the issue prevents completion of a task. Done
7 Write an accessibility conformance target into the dev backlog Track each defect as a ticket with a WCAG success criterion reference, owner and due date. Do not remediate off a PDF report with no ticketing trail. Done
8 Fix design-system-level issues once Contrast tokens, focus styles, form field patterns and button components should be fixed in the shared component library so fixes propagate and do not regress. Done
9 Do NOT rely on an accessibility overlay or widget Overlay/toolbar products (accessiBe, UserWay, AudioEye and similar) do not create legal compliance, have themselves been named in ADA lawsuits and an FTC enforcement action, and are opposed by most disability advocacy organizations. Native accessibility implemented. Done
10 Remediate downloadable documents Any PDF, whitepaper, case study, sample GIPS composite report or spreadsheet on the site must be tagged and accessible (reading order, alt text, table headers, document language, no scanned images of text). Done
11 Address AI-generated report output If the AI-powered reporting product emits PDFs or dashboards to clients, accessibility must be built into the generation template, not patched per-document. Done
12 Verify third-party embeds Cookie/consent banner, chat widget (Zoho SalesIQ), forms, calendar booking (Calendly), video player, Google Tag Manager-injected content, and social embeds verified for accessibility and vendor VPATs requested/confirmed. Done
C. VALIDATION & GOVERNANCE
13-25 Validation, VPAT, Statement, SLA & Ongoing Compliance Re-test after remediation, publish accessibility statement with SLA (5 business days), assign accountable owner, train team, retain evidence of good-faith effort. Done
